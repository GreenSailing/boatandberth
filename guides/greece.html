The checks

Buying
VAT paid

Proof that EU VAT was paid; registration alone is not proof.

What you need
VAT invoice, customs import declaration or T2L
What is at stake
24% of the boat's value
Off the charter licence

An ex-charter boat must leave the charter register; 24% VAT is then due on the value customs assess.

What you need
Deregistration certificate and VAT receipt
What is at stake
24% VAT plus fees
Survey

Not required by law; insurers want one ashore for boats over about 15 years.

What you need
An independent surveyor's report
What is at stake
€450–1,200, plus the haul-out
Sale agreement

Written contract, 10% deposit in a broker's or lawyer's escrow account, balance after survey, sea trial and clean title.

What you need
Agreement, escrow receipt, bill of sale
What is at stake
Broker's 4–6% is paid by the seller
Flag and papers

Greek flag needs a tax number (AFM) and 1–3 months; an EU flag such as Polish is online in 2–3 weeks.

What you need
Bill of sale, deletion from the old registry, VAT proof, CE papers
What is at stake
Greek €35–500; Polish €469 for life
Transfer tax

No stamp duty, but a Digital Transaction Duty (up to 2.4%) can apply to a contract signed in Greece. Ask a Greek tax adviser.

What you need
Bill of sale, filed with the registry
What is at stake
Up to 2.4% of the price
Owning
Cruising tax (TEPAI)

Boats over 7 m afloat in Greece, any flag; 10–12 m €33 a month, none while ashore. Check for unpaid arrears when buying.

What you need
eTEPAI receipt on board (myAADE)
What is at stake
The tax plus a fine: €400 (10–12 m), €1,100 (over 12 m)
Insurance

Third-party cover is compulsory over 7 m, with minimum limits (Law 4926/2022).

What you need
Certificate on board, in Greek or English
What is at stake
€100–400 a year third-party
Crew list

A signed crew list on board; a Transit Log for non-EU or non-VAT-paid boats.

What you need
Crew list, Transit Log if needed
What is at stake
Port police fines, often €500–1,500
Safety gear

Coast Guard list by length and area: lifejackets, flares, extinguishers, VHF and more.

What you need
The kit, with raft and flare service dates
What is at stake
€500–1,500 to complete a kit
VHF licence

The skipper needs an SRC to use a fixed DSC radio.

What you need
SRC certificate
What is at stake
Course €100–300
Skipper licence

The person in charge needs a recognised licence: an ICC, or a national certificate the Port Authority accepts. Check yours before the season.

What you need
ICC or national licence, with ID
What is at stake
Fines; insurance may not pay
Anchoring on seagrass

Anchoring on posidonia meadows is banned in protected areas and enforcement is growing; drop on sand.

What you need
A clear patch of sand
What is at stake
Fines, high in protected areas
Holding tank

No sewage discharge within 3 nm of land, or in harbours, bays and marine parks.

What you need
A working holding tank
What is at stake
Fines €500–5,000+
From outside the EU
Your boat's VAT status

EU VAT paid (Union status, stays for good) or Temporary Admission (no VAT, time-limited).

What you need
For Union status: VAT invoice or T2L. UK proofs issued before 2021 are no longer accepted; ask an EU customs office for a new one.
What is at stake
24% VAT on the boat's value if neither applies
Temporary Admission: 18 months

Non-EU resident, non-EU flag, private use: up to 18 months in the EU without VAT, then she must leave.

What you need
A non-EU resident can keep a boat that is NOT on an EU register in the EU without VAT for up to 18 months, private use only, then she must leave the EU. Greece records the entry date (unusually formal). Proof of the entry date: Transit Log, logbook, marina and fuel receipts. Ask customs before relying on a 'reset' (how long outside the EU is local practice).
What is at stake
Import VAT and duty if she overstays, also while laid up ashore
Buying a boat in Greece

Buy VAT-paid and keep the proof; otherwise 24% VAT, or out of the EU and back in on a non-EU flag.

What you need
Buy a VAT-paid boat and keep the proof: she keeps Union status whatever her flag. Buying a non-VAT-paid boat (e.g. still on charter) means paying 24% VAT, or taking her out of the EU and back in under Temporary Admission on a non-EU flag. The seller's VAT proof, in your name on the bill of sale
What is at stake
Without VAT proof you inherit the bill
Which flag

UK SSR (cheap, UK residents, no proof of title), UK Part 1 (proves ownership), or an EU flag (needs VAT paid).

What you need
UK: the Small Ships Register is cheap and quick but needs a UK resident owner and is not proof of title; Part 1 registration shows ownership and mortgages. Any non-EU flag means a Transit Log in Greece; an EU flag (Greek, Polish...) needs Union status. Registration certificate; the original Part 1 or SSR papers on board
What is at stake
Wrong flag for your plans = VAT or Transit Log problems
Transit Log

Every non-EU flagged boat in Greece; issued by the Port Authority on entry.

What you need
Non-EU flagged boats in Greece need a Transit Log, issued on entry by the Port Authority. Unlimited if she has Union status; limited if under Temporary Admission. Transit Log on board, stamped as required
What is at stake
Port police fines
Your own stay: 90 days in 180

90 days in any 180 across all Schengen countries; longer needs a Greek visa or residence permit.

What you need
UK and most non-EU citizens may stay 90 days in any 180 across the whole Schengen area, boat or not. Longer needs a Greek national visa or residence permit. The EU Entry/Exit System is replacing passport stamps; an ETIAS travel authorisation is due to start. Passport issued under 10 years ago, valid 3 months after you leave; keep a record of your days
What is at stake
Overstay: up to a 3-year Schengen ban
Skipper's papers

RYA ICC, with a Greek translation and the consular note; SRC for the VHF.

What you need
Greece expects a certificate of competence: for a UK flagged boat the RYA ICC is generally accepted. Greek rules also ask for a Greek translation and a consular note confirming the issuing body (the UK note is free from the British Embassy site). SRC for the VHF. ICC + Greek translation + consular note; SRC; ship radio licence
What is at stake
Fines or being stopped from sailing
Insurance

Cover for Greek waters, laid-up period and named skippers; Greek-law third-party limits.

What you need
UK and international yacht insurers cover Greece; check the cruising area, the laid-up period, named skippers, and that the third-party limits meet Greek law (Law 4926/2022). A Greek translation of the insurance certificate on board (your insurer provides it)
What is at stake
Fines; no cover if outside the policy's area
Cruising tax and tax at home

TEPAI as for everyone; over 183 days a year in Greece can make you Greek tax resident.

What you need
TEPAI applies to every boat over 7 m in Greek waters, any flag (Owning tab). Owning a boat does not by itself make you a Greek tax resident; living in Greece more than 183 days a year can. eTEPAI receipt; take advice if you spend long periods in Greece
What is at stake
TEPAI fines; double-tax questions
Bringing her home (UK)

An import into Great Britain: 20% VAT unless a relief applies (Returned Goods, Transfer of Residence).

What you need
Taking a boat into Great Britain is an import: VAT and duty are due on her value unless a relief applies, e.g. Returned Goods Relief for a boat that left the UK, or Transfer of Residence if you move back. Proof of UK status before she left; the RYA VAT Guide sets out the conditions
What is at stake
20% UK VAT on her value
Arriving by sea

From a non-EU port, clear in at a port of entry under the Q flag; clear out when you leave the EU.

What you need
From a non-EU port, clear in at a designated port of entry (or with Port Authority permission under the pleasure-craft rules), Q flag until cleared; clear out the same way when you leave the EU. Crew list and passports; courtesy flag in good condition
What is at stake
Days counted wrongly, or entry not recorded
