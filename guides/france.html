The checks

Buying
VAT paid (20%)

Proof that EU VAT was paid: a private sale adds no new VAT, so the proof comes from the chain of earlier papers. Ex-charter boats are often sold plus VAT.

What you need
Original VAT invoice, import declaration or a T2L / proof of Union status
What is at stake
20% of the boat's value
No transfer tax

France charges no transfer tax or stamp duty on a private sale of a used boat, and the change of owner is free.

What you need
Nothing to pay; keep the sale deed
What is at stake
None
Debts and mortgages

Since 2022 maritime mortgages on pleasure boats are kept by the clerk of the commercial court (greffe du tribunal de commerce), no longer by Customs. Ask for a statement before any deposit. Unpaid yearly boat tax and marina fees also follow the boat.

What you need
Mortgage statement (état des inscriptions) from the commercial court clerk
What is at stake
Debts follow the boat
Deposit and contract

The usual deposit is about 10%, subject to survey and sea trial. Say in writing whether it is an 'acompte' (both sides bound) or 'arrhes' (either may withdraw and lose it). The sale deed (acte de vente) should carry the hull number, the engine numbers and the price. The seller answers for hidden defects.

What you need
Signed sale deed (acte de vente) and deposit receipt
What is at stake
A deposit lost on a technicality
Change of owner

The seller starts the transfer online on Démarches Plaisance and gives the buyer a code; the buyer completes it. It must be declared within one month. There is no fee.

What you need
Sale deed, the boat's registration certificate, your ID and an address in France
What is at stake
She stays in the seller's name
Survey

A survey is not compulsory but insurers ask for one on older boats. Surveyors (experts maritimes) are not licensed by the state: choose one who belongs to a professional body and have her hauled out.

What you need
Survey report
What is at stake
Hidden defects, no insurance
Broker

Commission is usually 5% to 10% including VAT and normally paid by the seller. Ask before you view.

What you need
Written mandate or the broker's terms
What is at stake
A surprise bill at completion
Owning
Registration

Since 2022 one free online registration (enregistrement) replaces the old customs and maritime papers; the document is the certificat d'enregistrement. The French flag is open when at least half the boat belongs to EU or EEA citizens who live in France or give an address there. Older papers stay valid until something changes.

What you need
Certificat d'enregistrement in your name
What is at stake
Fines; trouble selling
Yearly boat tax (TAEMUP)

France's yearly boat tax (TAEMUP, the former DAFN) is paid by the owner of a French-flag boat of 7 m or more, and by French residents with a foreign-flag boat, online on Démarches Plaisance by 31 March. Hull part for 2026: 240 euros for 10 to 11 m, 274 for 11 to 12 m, 458 for 12 to 15 m. From 1 January 2027: 250, 285 and 470 euros, and 900 euros for 15 to 24 m. Engine part: in 2026 a small sum above 5 fiscal horsepower; from 2027 nothing below 120 kW, so a sailing boat's auxiliary engine is not taxed. Boats built before 1993 pay 80% less on the hull part, 1993 to 1997 55% less, 1998 to 2007 33% less. The official simulator gives the amount.

What you need
Payment receipt from Démarches Plaisance
What is at stake
Surcharges; the debt follows the boat
Insurance

Not compulsory by law for a private pleasure boat, but every marina asks for a third-party certificate, usually with wreck removal.

What you need
Insurance certificate (attestation)
What is at stake
No berth; unlimited personal liability
Skipper licence

No licence is needed to skipper a sailing boat in France. A motor boat, or a tender, with more than 4.5 kW (6 hp) needs the permis plaisance: the coastal option reaches 6 miles from a shelter, the offshore extension beyond. Visitors can use their own country's certificate or an ICC within its limits.

What you need
Permis plaisance for a motor boat or a powerful tender
What is at stake
Fines
Safety equipment by zone

On a French-flag boat the kit depends on the distance from a shelter: basic under 2 miles, coastal under 6, semi-offshore 6 to 60, offshore beyond. Three red hand flares from coastal; a liferaft, a fixed VHF and a harness and tether for each person from semi-offshore; an EPIRB offshore. A foreign-flag boat follows her flag's rules.

What you need
Kit for your zone, in date
What is at stake
Fines at inspection
Radio

A French-flag boat with a VHF needs a station licence and MMSI from the frequencies agency (ANFR), free. In French waters a fixed VHF needs the operator certificate (CRR) or the permis plaisance; a handheld of up to 6 W without DSC needs neither. Abroad the CRR is always needed.

What you need
ANFR licence, CRR or equivalent
What is at stake
Fines
Rules afloat

Five knots within 300 m of the shore. Anchoring on Posidonia seagrass is banned by local orders for boats of 20 or 24 m and over, and no boat may damage it: anchor on sand and check the Donia app. The national parks (Port-Cros, Calanques) and the Bonifacio reserve have their own anchoring and fishing rules. Since August 2026, driving a boat that needs the permis plaisance (a motor boat or a tender over 4.5 kW) with 0.8 g of alcohol per litre of blood, or 0.4 mg per litre of breath, is a crime: up to three years in prison and a 9,000-euro fine (Code des transports, article L5242-25). The law sets no figure for a sailing boat, but the skipper is still responsible.

What you need
Local orders for where you anchor
What is at stake
Heavy fines for seagrass damage
From outside the EU
Your boat's VAT status

EU VAT paid (Union status, stays for good) or Temporary Admission (no VAT, time-limited).

What you need
For Union status: VAT invoice or T2L. UK proofs issued before 2021 are no longer accepted; ask an EU customs office for a new one.
What is at stake
20% VAT on the boat's value if neither applies
Temporary Admission

A boat registered outside the EU and owned by someone established outside the EU may stay in the EU up to 18 months without VAT. Leaving EU waters and coming back starts a new period. She must not be lent or chartered to an EU resident.

What you need
Non-EU registration, proof you live outside the EU, entry record
What is at stake
VAT and duty become due
Importing a boat

Bringing a non-EU boat into free circulation in France costs 20% VAT on her customs value plus customs duty: none for a sailing boat classed as sea-going (a hull of 12 m or more), 1.7% for other sailing boats over 7.5 m.

What you need
Customs declaration through an agent
What is at stake
Using her without clearing is smuggling
Your own stay

Schengen allows 90 days in any 180 for visitors without a visa or residence permit. The boat's 18 months and your 90 days are separate clocks.

What you need
Passport stamps or the entry/exit record
What is at stake
Overstay fines and entry bans
Arriving from outside Schengen

Enter at a port that is a border crossing point and report to the border police; ask Customs for the current list. On the Mediterranean it includes Marseille, Toulon, Cannes, Antibes, Nice, Ajaccio, Bastia, Bonifacio and Calvi.

What you need
Passports and crew list
What is at stake
Fines for irregular entry
Living in France with a foreign flag

A French resident who owns or uses a foreign-flag boat of 7 m or more needs a 'passeport' from the maritime office and pays the same yearly tax as a French-flag boat.

What you need
Passeport and tax receipt
What is at stake
Tax plus surcharges
