The checks

Buying
VAT paid (21%)

Proof that EU VAT was paid: a private sale adds no new VAT, so the proof comes from the chain of earlier papers. Ex-charter boats are often sold plus VAT.

What you need
Original VAT invoice, import declaration or a T2L / proof of Union status
What is at stake
21% of the boat's value
Transfer tax (ITP)

A private second-hand sale pays transfer tax instead of VAT, to the buyer's region: 5% in Catalonia, 4% in the Balearics and Murcia, 6% in the Valencia region, and in Andalusia 4% up to 8 m and 8% over 8 m (rates change: confirm with the regional tax office). It is charged on the higher of the price and the tax office's own valuation tables.

What you need
Tax form (modelo 600, 620 or 621) paid: within one month in Catalonia and the Balearics, 30 working days in Murcia, two months in Andalusia
What is at stake
The change of ownership is refused; surcharges if late
Matriculation tax (12%)

Spain charges a one-off 12% tax (IEDMT) on boats over 8 m when first registered in Spain, or when a Spanish tax resident uses a foreign-flag boat here. A boat already on the Spanish register has paid it; ask for the proof (modelo 576). Buying a foreign-flag boat as a resident triggers it, valued by the official tables.

What you need
Modelo 576 showing the tax paid or exempt
What is at stake
12% of the boat's official value, plus penalties
Debts and mortgages

Mortgages, embargoes and leasing on a boat are entered in the movable property registry (Registro de Bienes Muebles). Ask for a nota simple before any deposit, and for the boat's registry sheet from the maritime office.

What you need
Nota simple from the Registro de Bienes Muebles
What is at stake
Debts follow the boat
Deposit and contract

The usual deposit (arras or señal) is about 10%, subject to survey and sea trial. The contract should carry the hull number, the engine numbers and the price. In a private sale the seller answers for hidden defects for six months.

What you need
Signed contract (contrato de compraventa) and deposit receipt
What is at stake
A deposit lost on a technicality
Change of ownership

After the transfer tax is paid, file the change at the maritime office (Capitanía Marítima or Distrito Marítimo) or online, within three months. The fee is about 19 euros; a nautical agency (gestoría) can do the whole file.

What you need
Contract, transfer tax receipt, the boat's papers, your ID and NIE
What is at stake
She stays in the seller's name
Broker

Commission is usually 5% to 10% plus VAT and normally paid by the seller. Ask before you view.

What you need
Written mandate or the broker's terms
What is at stake
A surprise bill at completion
Owning
Registration and inspection

Private boats are on List 7 (Lista 7ª). CE-marked boats up to 12 m can use the simpler inscripción; larger ones need full registration with a Permiso de Navegación. Spanish-flag boats of 6 to 24 m pass a technical inspection (ITB) every 5 years.

What you need
Permiso de Navegación or Certificado de Inscripción, Certificado de Navegabilidad in date
What is at stake
Fines; the insurer may refuse a claim
Third-party insurance

Compulsory for every boat with an engine, and for boats without one over 6 m. Minimum cover about 120,000 euros per injured person (240,000 per accident) and 96,000 for damage.

What you need
Policy and payment receipt on board
What is at stake
Fines and the boat held
Skipper licence

On a Spanish-flag sailing boat up to 15 m you need at least the PER with its sailing practice, good to 12 miles from the coast; the PNB only covers boats up to 8 m. On a foreign-flag boat the flag state's rules apply. A citizen of the EEA, the UK or another country listed in the licence decree can skipper a Spanish-flag boat on a licence from their own country of nationality or residence, once the maritime office (Capitanía Marítima) authorises it. For RYA certificates the decree sets the limits: Day Skipper up to 24 m and 12 miles from the coast, Coastal Skipper 20 miles, Yachtmaster Offshore 150 miles, Yachtmaster Ocean unlimited; an ICC as written on it.

What you need
Licence or certificate that your flag requires
What is at stake
Fines; insurance may not pay
Safety equipment by zone

The required kit depends on the navigation zone, from zone 7 (sheltered waters) to zone 1 (unlimited); zone 4 is up to 12 miles. A liferaft and an EPIRB are required only beyond 12 miles (zones 1 to 3).

What you need
Kit for your zone, in date
What is at stake
Fines at inspection
Foreign flag and living in Spain

Non-residents can keep a foreign-flag boat in Spain. A Spanish tax resident who uses a foreign-flag boat over 8 m here owes the 12% matriculation tax. Light registrations are examined closely: the Dutch ICP in particular has been refused as proof of flag.

What you need
Modelo 576, and a proper flag-state registration
What is at stake
12% tax plus penalties
Lighthouse fee (T-0)

Spain's navigation-aids fee (tasa de ayudas a la navegación, T-0) is collected by the port authority, often through the marina bill. Sailing boats up to 12 m do not pay. A sailing boat over 12 m based in a Spanish port pays each year 0.57 euros x length x beam x 16: about 456 euros for 12.5 m by 4 m (ports law, article 240).

What you need
Marina or port authority receipt
What is at stake
Added to the marina bill
Rules afloat

In the Balearics anchoring on Posidonia seagrass is banned: anchor on sand only and use the official maps. Keep out of buoyed bathing zones; off unbuoyed beaches the first 200 m is a bathing zone, crossed only at 3 knots and straight in or out. Skippering while drunk is punishable; the law gives no figure for leisure skippers.

What you need
The Posidonia map for where you anchor
What is at stake
Heavy fines in the Balearics
From outside the EU
Your boat's VAT status

EU VAT paid (Union status, stays for good) or Temporary Admission (no VAT, time-limited).

What you need
For Union status: VAT invoice or T2L. UK proofs issued before 2021 are no longer accepted; ask an EU customs office for a new one.
What is at stake
21% VAT on the boat's value if neither applies
Temporary Admission

A boat registered outside the EU and owned by someone established outside the EU may stay in the EU up to 18 months without VAT. Leaving EU waters and coming back starts a new period. She must not be lent or chartered to an EU resident.

What you need
Non-EU registration, proof you live outside the EU, entry record
What is at stake
VAT and duty become due
Importing a boat

Bringing a non-EU boat into free circulation in Spain costs 21% VAT on her customs value plus customs duty: none for a sailing boat classed as sea-going (a hull of 12 m or more), 1.7% for other sailing boats over 7.5 m. A resident importing a boat over 8 m also owes the 12% matriculation tax.

What you need
Customs declaration through an agent
What is at stake
Using her without clearing is smuggling
Your own stay

Schengen allows 90 days in any 180 for visitors without a visa or residence permit. The boat's 18 months and your 90 days are separate clocks.

What you need
Passport stamps or the entry/exit record
What is at stake
Overstay fines and entry bans
Becoming resident

Spending more than 183 days a year in Spain normally makes you a tax resident. From then a foreign-flag boat over 8 m that you use here falls under the 12% matriculation tax: take advice before you cross that line.

What you need
Advice from a gestoría or tax adviser
What is at stake
12% of the boat's official value
Selling or taking her home

To move a Spanish-registered boat to another flag, ask the maritime office to take her off the register (baja) and for the certificate the new flag will want; clear any mortgage first.

What you need
Certificate of deletion (baja), papers returned
What is at stake
The new flag cannot register her
